01
Why this matters

What must this team do to survive?

“The task it must perform if it is to survive.

Eric Miller, Tavistock Institute

This is where the idea comes from. The Tavistock Institute developed the primary task to explain why capable, well-led teams have less impact than they should. When a team cannot say what it must do to survive, it tries to do everything and loses focus.

Survival is different from aspiration. When teams think about survival, they focus on what they must deliver, who depends on it, and what would be lost without them. Every asset is tested this way at portfolio review — so is every team around it.

02
The framework

The primary task

In a paper company, everyone makes, sells and ships paper, so the shared task is obvious. This team brings different specialists together — like a mechanic, a chef, a dentist and a pilot. A team like this needs to write its shared task down.

01

This team exists to…

the outcome we must deliver to survive

02

by means of…

the contribution only we can make

03

in order to…

the wider purpose it serves

Example · Airline cabin crew

Exists to bring passengers back to fly with the airline again, by means of keeping them safe and making the flight easy, in order to protect the airline’s profit in a market where flights are very similar.

03
Exercise · Individual

On your own

You will each be given a card. Complete the sentence for this team as it works today:

5 min — write → 5 min — read out & discuss
04
Exercise · Pairs, then room

What our week says

Think about a typical week:

  • What takes up most of your time?
  • Who do you work with most closely, inside and outside this team?
  • Which meetings do you attend, and who sets their agenda?
  • When does another function ask for your input?
  • Which decisions have you been part of recently?
In pairs · 10 min

Someone watches this team for three months. They never see our mission statement. What would they say we are here to do?

As a room · 5 min

How close is that to the cards? What would the asset leadership team say?

05
Exercise · Groups

The survival test

Imagine the asset leadership team removes this sub-team at the next resourcing round. Twelve months later, what has the asset lost?

Use the language the asset leadership team uses: label, launch, access, payer evidence, timelines, lifecycle, competitive position.

  • 7 of 9 stakeholders name Benefit–Risk as where we add most value
  • Safety input helped defend the labels worldwide
  • A fatigue and itch endpoint was added to a benefit–risk assessment
  • Patient input moved an asset leadership discussion beyond price
  • Fatigue was not measured in HS, and payers later asked for it
  • A phase IV study finished six months early
  • We can bring a patient organisation to UCB within one or two weeks
12 min — groups, worksheet → 8 min — feedback, top three
06
Worked example

An in-house legal team

If the company used an outside law firm instead, what would the business lose?

Before

Protect the company from legal risk.

→
After

Help the business trade faster and more profitably, by means of bringing legal judgement into decisions early, in order to take the right risks and avoid the wrong ones.

Protecting the company is still in the sentence — it moved to “in order to.”

08
Exercise · Twos or threes

Draft it

Write one sentence that describes what the team does, in words every function can use.

Start from the survival list: what does the asset need? You can use words from the examples and from other groups.

10 min — draft → 4 min — dot vote

Each person places three dots on the strongest words and phrases across all the drafts.

09
Exercise · As a room

Build and test

8 min — build one sentence from the words with the most dots

Then 3 minutes — vote on each question. Hold up 0 to 5 fingers: 0 means no, 5 means yes, completely.

Q1

Would the asset leadership team recognise their asset in it?

Q2

Can every function see its work in “by means of”?

Q3

Does it tell us what to do when regulatory and patient work conflict?

Q4

Would someone watching us see it in how we work?

10
Exercise · Whole room, 3 rounds

The Patient Voice Relay

I will read this to you. You then have 1 minute to plan before the timer starts. We will not answer any questions.

Your task, as a group, is to show how the patient voice (the ball) can move through your team as efficiently as possible to reach the asset decision.

Rules of play
01

You cannot pass the ball to the person next to you.

02

Every team member must touch the ball.

03

The ball must travel through the air.

04

The ball cannot be rolled across surfaces — for example the floor, walls, tables or chairs.

05

Remember the order, because you will repeat it in the next rounds.

06

If the ball hits the ground, or the order is not followed, you will get a time penalty.
